# Electricity

Electric machines are faster and need no fuel in front of them. Energy is measured in kilojoules (kJ), power in watts.

## Generating Power

**Electric Engine** — feed it kinetic force on its front face and it puts out electricity at 400 V on the other faces. Any KF source works: a Kinetic Force Generator, steam, or later something bigger.

**Solar Panel** — free power in daylight, up to 20 kW.

## Carrying Power

**Electric Wire** connects generators to machines. **Power Relays** branch the network. Long lines lose power as heat — thicker conductors and higher voltage lose less.

**Electric Capacitor** — stores up to 50 MJ to smooth out supply.

**Power Transformer** — steps voltage up or down.

Engines and transformers make alternating current (AC); solar panels and capacitors make direct current (DC). A Rectifier turns AC into DC, an Inverter DC into AC.

## Checking Your Grid

Right-click any wire or machine with a **Multimeter** to see voltage, power flow and losses.

## Your First Electric Machines

The **Electric Furnace** smelts without fuel. The **Electric Heater** reaches any temperature you set. Most advanced machines are built from a **Basic Machine Casing** or **Machine Casing** plus circuit boards — see Electronics.
