# Heat

Many machines need heat: the Boiler, Smelter, Foundry, Glass Blowing Station, Chemical Reactor and others. Every one shows its temperature in its window.

## The Heater

Your first heat source. Put fuel in it and it stores heat, then pushes that heat into every machine it touches — place it right next to, under or above the machine.

Fuel decides how hot it can get:

- Sticks and planks: up to about 200 °C

- Coal, charcoal, coal block: up to about 300 °C

- Coke coal: up to about 450 °C

- Blaze rod: up to about 500 °C

- Lava bucket: up to about 800 °C (gives the bucket back)

A machine fed by a Heater keeps warming up while the Heater burns, so it can climb well past the Heater's own temperature — just keep the fuel coming.

## Moving Heat

**Thermal Conductors** carry heat over a distance, like a heat pipe. Chain them from a Heater to machines further away.

A **Radiator** dumps unwanted heat into the air; connect it to a conductor from its back side.

## Later Heat Sources

The **Electric Heater** reaches any temperature you set, using electricity. The **Heat Exchanger** moves heat between two fluid streams.

## Coke

Coal and charcoal bake into coke coal in the **Coke Oven**: build a 3×3×3 hollow cube of 26 Coke Bricks (leave the middle empty) and it forms when the last brick goes in. Coke burns hotter, and you need it later to make silicon. The oven also collects creosote.
